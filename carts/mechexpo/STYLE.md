# Mech Expo 2000: style guide

The pitch is "Mech Expo 2000 — MVP and Future Work" (2026-10-11). This page is the look and
feel every model, animation, screen and sound for the cart is checked against.

**The frame is a trade show, not a war.** Every fight is a product demonstration at the year
2000's big expo, run for the UAPC's defence contract. The mechs are demo units fresh off the
show floor: clean paint, polished chrome, maker's emblems, serial numbers, hazard stripes where
an engineer would put them. They are machines a company is proud of and wants to sell, and
each one should look like its maker's best foot forward. Wear and salvage belong to Heartland
Atomic alone, and come later. The mood is super robot fun, bright and confident; nobody is
piloting, so a mech's head is a face, and its pose is its personality.

**Era: late arcade and early Dreamcast, around 1999.** The references are the 3D fighters and
robot games of that time seen side-on: Cyber Troopers Virtual-On's toy-like heroic mechs, the
chunky clarity of Power Stone and Rival Schools, the clean armour of Zone of the Enders, and
the translucent-plastic, brushed-chrome, blue-white glow of Y2K consumer electronics. Shapes
are big, angular and few, with colour blocking doing most of the work; panel lines and greebles
are a garnish, not the meal. Low polygon counts are part of the look, not a limit to hide.

**Read from the side, at a distance.** The camera is fixed and side-on and zooms out as the
mechs separate, so a mech must read as a silhouette at about 80 pixels tall on a 320×240
screen. Proportions are heroic: a broad chest and shoulders, a narrow waist, solid legs planted
wide, a small head. The weapon is part of the silhouette and must be readable at that size; the
Hero's sword is long and bright. All mechs are roughly the same size, about 12 m tall standing
(1 unit = 1 m, Y up): big enough to tower over the crowd and the booths, small enough that the
expo around them stays readable. Front and back of each mech differ clearly, since both facings are seen.

**Built from rigid parts.** Mechs are animated as rigid parts turning at joints, with no
skinning, so joints are design features rather than seams to hide: ball joints, hinge drums,
pistons and armour plates that overlap so a bent elbow or knee still looks whole. Each part's
origin is its joint, as with the movement garden's robot. An echo is the same frame reworked by
a rival, so the base mech's parts should be designed to take new paint, new weapons and a few
new plates.

**Colour belongs to the company.** Momentum Robotics is red with white accents and chrome trim;
Umbral Research is black and violet; Raikō is yellow; Heartland is faded forest green with
primer grey; Binary Mechanica is white, silver and cyan in translucent plastic. Keep each mech
to its company's two or three colours plus a neutral metal, with one small glow (eyes, a
reactor, a vent) as the accent. The stage stays cooler and quieter than the mechs: a grey-blue
hangar under white floodlights, with the expo's banners and booths as the only strong colour
behind the fighters.

**Weight in motion and sound.** These are tonnes of metal: slow walks, short jumps with a
heavy landing, clear anticipation before a swing, strong hitstop and screen shake on contact.
Menus are an expo brochure with translucent panels, catalogue spec sheets, lanyards and booth
numbers, set in the repository's OFL fonts (`tools/fonts/`). The music is Y2K electronic, big
beat and drum and bass; the sound effects are servos, hydraulics and heavy impacts in hardware
reverb.
